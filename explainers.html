Notes

The deal types, in plain English.

Short definitions for investors who don't work in real estate. They describe how these transactions generally work, not any specific deal.

Note 1

Earnest money deposit (EMD)

When a buyer signs a purchase contract, they usually put down a deposit to show the seller they're serious. It's held by the title company until closing.

Short-term capital can cover that deposit for a buyer who is short on time or cash, and comes back when the deal closes or under the contract terms.

Note 2

Double close

In a double close, a property is bought and resold in two back-to-back closings, often on the same day. The middle buyer needs funds for the first closing before the second buyer's money arrives.

Transactional capital covers that first closing and is repaid from the second.

Note 3

How escrow works

A title or escrow company is a neutral third party. It holds money and documents and releases them only when the conditions in the closing instructions are met.

Escrow reduces some risks but does not remove them; closings can be delayed or fail.
